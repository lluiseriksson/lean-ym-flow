### The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow I

- Source: [arXiv:2606.21062v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21062v2); Xiong, Zhiyao, Yang, Xaokui, Yau, Shing-Tung.
- Dates: first 2026-06-19; latest 2026-09-05. **revised-in-trailing-week**.
- Evidence: **literature / preprint**. Reviewed: Abstract and submission history.
- Reported result: The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow paper establishes convergence for a class of prescribed Hermitian tensors; a parabolic comparison principle supplies the uniform C0 estimate.
- Limits: This is a flow of Hermitian metrics on holomorphic bundles, not Wilson lattice gradient flow. Read the precise tensor and manifold assumptions before importing a comparison principle. Version 2 is dated 5 September; version 1 is from June.

### The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow II

- Source: [arXiv:2606.21073v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21073v1); Xiong, Zhiyao, Yang, Xiaokui, Yau, Shing-Tung.
- Dates: first 2026-06-19; latest 2026-06-19. **background**.
- Evidence: **literature / preprint**. Reviewed: Abstract and submission history.
- Reported result: The second prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills flow paper gives a Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau analogue with an explicit subsheaf degree condition and convergence to a prescribed curvature tensor.
- Limits: Its subsheaf and positivity hypotheses cannot be dropped or replaced by ordinary Wilson-action monotonicity. First posted in June; included only as a companion to the newly revised first paper.

### Thick points of log-correlated Gaussian fields do not depend on the mollifier

- Source: [arXiv:2609.08132v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08132v1); Ambrose, Kyle.
- Dates: first 2026-09-08; latest 2026-09-08. **new-this-calendar-week**.
- Evidence: **literature / preprint**. Reviewed: Abstract and submission history.
- Reported result: Ambrose reports almost-sure independence of thick-point sets from admissible mollifiers for log-correlated Gaussian fields, including a two-dimensional domain GFF extension.
- Limits: Admissibility conditions and the distribution-valued field matter. This is not a dimension-uniform finite massive covariance-decay theorem or a Wilson-flow smoothing bound.
